What this is

Each stroke is authored in a table of coaching phases — where the hands are, where the racquet points, how far the shoulders have turned ahead of the hips — and the elbows, knees and racquet tip are solved rather than drawn. The result is baked to joint coordinates: seventeen positions per frame at 60 fps. The page projects them to 2D itself, which is why the camera orbits and why all eight strokes together cost 91 KB.

The numbers above are measured from the animation, not typed in, and every one is checked against a target band. Racquet-tip speed at impact and shoulder–hip separation come from published biomechanics; the rest are coaching consensus and are marked provisional in the build report, because a band nobody measured is a guess wearing a lab coat.

Two things the harness caught that no amount of looking would have: the racquet was silently changing length between keyframes, and the interpolation was braking the racquet at the exact moment of impact — peak speed was arriving 42 ms early. Hand-drawn animation never tells you either.
